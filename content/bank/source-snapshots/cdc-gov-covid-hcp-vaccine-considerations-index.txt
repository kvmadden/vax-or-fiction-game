Interim Clinical Considerations for Use of COVID-19 Vaccines in the United States
For Health Care Providers
Sept. 23, 2026
WHAT TO KNOW
Because of legal uncertainties and inquiries, the CDC states the recommendations for COVID-19 vaccination from the July 2025 immunization schedule remain in effect for the 2026-2027 respiratory illness season.
This page has been updated to reflect current FDA regulatory information for the 2026-2027 COVID-19 vaccines, including approved products and asterisks indicating where recommendations for use are off-label, formulation, age and risk indications, dosage, and contraindications.
As with all medical decision making between patients and their healthcare providers, all patients should discuss the benefits and risks of COVID-19 vaccination to make informed and personalized decisions about vaccination.
At a glance
These clinical considerations provide guidance for healthcare professionals and public health officials concerning the use of COVID-19 vaccines in the United States for the 2026-2027 season. The recommendations in this document reflect CDC’s adult and pediatric immunization schedule as of July 2025.
The Table of Contents on each page can be used to navigate to all guidance pages.
Summary of recent changes
Last updated September 23, 2025
A 2026-2027 COVID-19 vaccine is recommended for all adults ages 18 years and older in the United States for the prevention of COVID-19.
A 2026-2027 COVID-19 vaccine is also recommended for all children ages 6 months–17 years with moderate to severe immunocompromise; for all other children, a COVID-19 vaccine is recommended through shared clinical decision-making.
Where a recommendation on this page describes use that is not within the FDA-approved indication, the page identifies it as off-label.
Pfizer-BioNTech COVID-19 Vaccine is no longer authorized for children ages 6 months–4 years. The only FDA-approved COVID-19 vaccine available for this age group is Moderna (Spikevax).
